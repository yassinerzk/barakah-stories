Privacy Policy — Barakah Stories

Last updated: 23 November 2026

Barakah Stories is made by Klay Creative Lab LLC. This policy explains what the app does with your information. The short version: the app works fully without an account and keeps everything on your device. If you choose to sign up, we store only your email, your saved posts and your reading progress, so they follow you between devices — and you can delete all of it at any time.

What we collect

We do not use analytics to study how you use the app, and we do not sell personal information. The free version of the app shows advertising; Barakah Pro shows none. What advertising involves is under "Advertising" below, and how we measure our own ads on Facebook and Instagram is under "Measuring our own ads".

You can use the whole app without an account. If you never sign up, everything you create — your story designs, saved posts, recently opened posts, your language, theme and prayer settings, and your Quran reading progress — is stored only on your device, in the app's own storage, and is removed when you uninstall the app. We receive nothing.

If you create an account

Signing up is optional. It exists for one purpose: keeping your saved posts and Quran reading progress available on more than one device.

If you do sign up, we store:

  • Your email address, used to identify your account and let you sign in.
  • Your saved story designs and your Quran reading progress, so they can be synced.

Your password is never visible to us. Accounts run on Supabase, which processes this data on our behalf as our hosting provider; it is not sold or shared with anyone else. Your data is protected so that only your own account can read or write it.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account at any time, and doing so permanently removes your account, your synced posts and your synced reading progress. It cannot be undone.

  • In the app: Me → Account → Delete account.
  • On the web: use the delete-account page, sign in, and confirm. You do not need to reinstall the app to do this.

Posts saved on your device stay on your device; uninstalling the app removes those.

Location

If you open the Prayer screen and choose to use your location, the app asks for location permission. Your coordinates are used to calculate prayer times and the direction of the Qibla, which happens entirely on your device, and to look up the name of your city or area so the screen can show where the times apply.

That place-name lookup is performed by your phone's own operating system service (Google Play Services on Android, Apple's location services on iOS). Your coordinates are passed to that service by the operating system, and its own privacy policy applies to that lookup. We never receive your coordinates, and we do not store or transmit them anywhere else.

Location is only ever read while the app is open and you are using it. The app does not track you in the background. You can refuse the permission and still use the app — choose one of the built-in cities instead, and no location data is read at all. You can revoke the permission at any time in your device settings.

The app also reads your phone's compass sensor to rotate the Qibla dial. That reading stays on the device.

Internet connections the app makes

The app goes online for content. Apart from the ad measurement described under "Measuring our own ads", it never reports on you:

  • Hadith. The hadith collections (Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim) are downloaded the first time you open the Hadith section, from the public jsDelivr content delivery network, and then kept on your device so they work offline.
  • Audio stories. The list of stories, their pictures and the free preview of each story come from our content server (hosted on Supabase) or our public website. A story you download is saved on your device so it plays offline. When a Pro member plays or downloads a full story, the app sends the random purchase identifier described under "Purchases" to our server, which asks RevenueCat whether Pro is active and then returns a link that works for a short time. We do not keep a record of which stories you listen to.

As with visiting any website, the servers that deliver these files receive your device's IP address in order to send a response, and may keep it briefly in their routine logs. We do not use it to identify you.

The Quran text and translation are bundled inside the app and require no connection.

Reminders, prayer alerts and widgets

You can turn on prayer alerts (with the adhan sound of your choice) and a daily reminder, and choose when they arrive. They are scheduled by your phone, on your phone, from the prayer times it calculates itself. There is no server involved, we are not told that you turned them on, and no notification is ever sent to you by us. You can change them in the app and switch the app's notifications off in your phone settings.

Home Screen widgets show your prayer times and other content from the app's own storage on your device. Nothing is sent anywhere to fill them.

Purchases

Barakah Pro and the optional "Support the app" tips are bought through Google Play (or the App Store on iPhone). We never see your card or payment details; the store handles the payment under its own privacy policy.

To know whether your phone has Pro, the app uses RevenueCat, which processes purchases on our behalf. It receives your purchase record from the store (what was bought, when, and whether it is still active) linked to a random identifier created by the app, not your name or email. RevenueCat's own privacy policy applies to that processing. We use this to unlock Pro and restore it on a new phone, and RevenueCat also tells Meta when a trial or purchase happens, as described under "Measuring our own ads". It is not sold to anyone.

Advertising

People who use the app without Barakah Pro see advertising from Google AdMob: now and then a full-screen ad when the app opens or when you start a new story, a banner on the Home and Me screens, and an optional video ad you can choose to watch in exchange for a small reward (such as removing the app name from a story). There are no ads on the Quran, hadith or prayer screens, and none at all with Barakah Pro.

To show an ad, Google's software in the app receives your device's advertising identifier, your approximate location derived from your internet address, and basic information about your device and how you interact with the ad. We ask Google for ads that are not personalised to you, and for ads suitable for all ages. Where the law requires it (the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland), the app asks for your consent before the first ad. Google's policy explains how it uses this information. You can reset or delete the advertising identifier in your phone's settings.

On iPhone and iPad, the app never asks for permission to track you, so Apple does not give Google the advertising identifier there. Nothing about you is used to track you across other companies' apps or websites, on any platform.

Measuring our own ads

We advertise Barakah Stories on Facebook and Instagram, and we want to know which of those ads lead people to install the app and to try or buy Barakah Pro. For that, the app includes Meta's software (the Meta SDK), and RevenueCat, which handles purchases for us, reports purchases to Meta.

  • From the app, Meta receives that the app was installed and opened, with a random identifier that Meta's software creates for this installation, basic information about your device (such as its model, system version, language and time zone) and, as with any internet connection, your IP address.
  • From RevenueCat, Meta receives when a free trial starts, a subscription starts or renews, or a purchase or tip is made, with the product's price and currency, linked to that same random identifier.
  • Meta never receives your name, email or account, your advertising identifier, your location, or anything you read, listen to or create in the app. On iPhone and iPad the app never asks for permission to track you, and it tells Meta that tracking is not allowed.

Where the law requires consent (the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland), none of this happens unless you allowed "Store and/or access information on a device" and "Measure advertising performance" in the consent message the app shows before ads. You can change your choice at any time under Me → Privacy choices. If you withdraw it, the app stops Meta's software from reporting and removes the identifier from RevenueCat, so later renewals are no longer reported.

Meta's privacy policy applies to what Meta receives.

Sharing your stories

When you share a story, the app creates an image on your device and hands it to your phone's normal share sheet. You choose where it goes — WhatsApp, another app, or your photo library. The image never passes through us.

Children

The app is not directed at children and we do not knowingly collect information from them. There is nothing to collect.

Security

Your content stays on your device, so its security is governed by your device's own protections, such as your screen lock and device encryption. Everything the app sends or receives over the internet is encrypted in transit (HTTPS).

Changes to this policy

If a future version of the app adds features that handle data differently, this policy will be updated before that version is released, and the date at the top will change.
